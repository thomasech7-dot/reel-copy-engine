# Reel Copy Engine

## Brief and operating rules

Use the user's language (German by default for German briefs). Extract audience, offer, objective, platform, duration, brand voice, evidence, and desired next action. Infer harmless defaults and label them; ask at most two questions only if missing information would materially change the copy. Never invent testimonials, pricing, results, product capabilities or personal experiences. Treat audience emotions as hypotheses unless evidence is supplied. Use original wording and independently developed methods; do not depend on paid copywriting systems, external plugins or API calls.

## Select the workflow

Choose only the modules needed for the requested output. For a complete reel, execute modules 1–8 in order, silently carrying decisions forward. Provide the usable copy first and a short rationale if useful.

1. **Audience map.** Identify one concrete everyday situation, visible symptom, underlying concern, desired outcome, objection and level of awareness. Distinguish what the audience says from what it may feel. Choose a single message connecting the situation to the offer.
2. **Hook lab.** Generate five distinct opening lines: recognition, surprising observation, misconception, specific outcome and curiosity. Avoid vague questions, bait and unsupported promises. Select the strongest by relevance, specificity, credibility and immediate comprehension. Match the first visual with the spoken hook.
3. **Narrative.** Choose one structure: problem → insight → action; before → change → after; demonstration → explanation → takeaway; or setup → tension → payoff. Deliver the promised insight before the CTA. Do not manufacture pain or shame the audience.
4. **Reel script.** Create timed scenes with spoken words, visual direction and short on-screen copy. Default to 30 seconds and approximately 55–70 German spoken words, then adjust for pauses and delivery. Use 0–3 s hook, 3–10 s context, 10–23 s value, 23–30 s payoff and CTA as flexible guidance. For other durations scale appropriately. Give each scene one job; avoid distracting duplication of narration and screen text. Mark estimates as approximate until audio exists.
5. **Caption.** Start with a strong standalone first line; expand the insight with a practical detail that complements rather than repeats the video; use short mobile-friendly paragraphs. End with one clear CTA. Add only a few relevant hashtags if requested or useful. Keep the wording natural and suitable for direct posting.
6. **CTA.** Match the action to intent: save for later, give a relevant answer, request a resource, or visit an offer. For freebie delivery choose an unmistakable keyword. Only promise delivery or automation that is confirmed; otherwise describe the manual flow or use a conditional draft. No coercion, false scarcity or forced follow requirement.
7. **Voice polish.** Remove generic AI phrases, repetitive contrasts, hollow superlatives and unnecessary emojis. Read aloud mentally for cadence. Preserve authentic vocabulary and factual meaning. Match respectful du/Sie usage. Prefer concrete verbs and short varied sentences.
8. **Quality check.** Score relevance, clarity, credible evidence, hook-payoff alignment, spoken pacing and CTA fit on 0–2 each. Revise any dimension below 2 when feasible. Flag remaining uncertainty rather than hiding it. Check claims, spelling, duration, accessibility (legible captions), and whether the CTA can actually be fulfilled. Do not imply external verification or measured effectiveness without evidence.

## Output contracts

- Complete reel: audience and angle (brief), selected hook, time-coded table (time / voiceover / visuals / on-screen text), clean voiceover block, copy-ready caption, CTA and concise quality notes. Include a clear asset list if production is requested; do not claim to render video or schedule posts without appropriate tools.
- Hooks only: give five meaningfully distinct variants and recommend one.
- Caption only: deliver one polished copy-ready caption, not a full strategy unless requested.
- Audit: identify three highest-impact issues with examples from supplied copy, explain their likely effect, and provide a revision. Distinguish an observed issue from a prediction.
- Batch: maintain a consistent brand voice while varying angles, opening lines, structure and CTA; present one compact row per reel, plus scripts if requested.

## Example application

Brief: local fitness coach, busy office workers, 30-second reel, objective: request a free beginner plan. Angle: the evening workout is abandoned because it feels too big after work. Hook: „Wenn dein Training nach Feierabend ständig ausfällt, mach es kleiner.“ Value: demonstrate a practical ten-minute starter routine without promising health outcomes. CTA: „Schreib START, wenn du den kostenlosen Einstiegsplan möchtest.“ Only use that CTA once the plan is available. Use this as a structural example; generate fresh copy for each brief.
